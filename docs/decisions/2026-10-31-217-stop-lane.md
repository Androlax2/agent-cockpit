# Stops wait in a lane of their own: two workers, two waiting places

Issue #217. Decided by Theo (option B in the issue); the sizes and the routing of each
command by the implementing session.

- **`session-end`, `session-sleep` and `resource-stop` run in the stop lane.** They only stop
  an agent or an execution, and a stop may hold its worker through the grace and kill wait
  (up to 10 s for a session, 20 s for an execution). In the bulk lane they waited behind
  launches and were answered `busy` when it was full; in the control lane eight slow stops
  would have taken input and status away.
- **`session-restart` and `session-restart-all` stay in the bulk lane.** They stop and then
  launch, and a launch is bulk work.
- **Two workers, two waiting.** Two stops run at once, so one agent that ignores `SIGTERM`
  does not block the next stop. The waiting places are few on purpose: the last session stop
  admitted is answered within 20 s, inside the TUI's 30 s request deadline. A deeper queue
  would let a client time out while the daemon still runs its stop; a full stop lane answers
  `busy` at once, naming it, like the other lanes.
- **Rejected:** stops in the control lane (option A), because a slow stop would hold a
  control worker for the whole escalation; keeping stops in bulk and amending report 03
  (option C), because report 15 scenario 3 requires stops served under load.

Evidence: `crates/cockpit-daemon/tests/stop_lane.rs`.

## Revisited 2026-10-31 (#230)

Decided by the worker: the rule above, that a stop "neither holds a control worker nor waits
behind launches", leaves three options in the issue, and only A and B keep it; B is the one
an M1 fix can carry. A stop of a session whose launch still runs is refused with `conflict`,
never queued behind it.

- **Refuse, do not wait.** `end` and `sleep` take the session's lock through
  `Sessions::lock_to_stop` (`crates/cockpit-sessions/src/lifecycle.rs`). While a launch holds
  that lock (start, wake, resume, restart, a start-file restart, the periodic pass's pending
  restart) they answer `Error::LaunchInProgress`, `conflict` on the wire, at once. The stop
  lane's two workers are freed immediately, whatever a provider's startup takes.
- **The launch mechanism sets the marker.** `Sessions::launch` and a restart's `stop` take
  the held lock (`Operation`) and mark it themselves, so no launch path can run unlocked or
  unmarked. An operation refused before it reaches them (a wake of a running session, a
  resume that cannot resume) refuses no stop. A restart is marked through its stop phase
  too: it relaunches right after, so the message names a launch or a restart.
- **A launch that starts while a stop waits refuses that stop too.** A stop queued behind a
  short holder (the periodic pass, a link, an `ensure_awake` on a running session) is woken
  when that holder turns into a launch, and refused, so it never ends up behind the launch.
  The cost: a stop that was ahead of a launch in the lock's queue may be refused although it
  would have run first. A retry serves it.
- **The marker is in memory.** Only a running launch counts; a session left `launching` by an
  error or a crash has no marker, so `end` still stops it as before (#157, #221).
- **Rejected:** A, pre-empting a running launch at a checkpoint: it needs cancellation points
  through the provider, process and tmux steps, wider than an M1 fix. C, accepting the wait:
  it contradicts report 03's stop-lane rule.

Evidence: `crates/cockpit-daemon/tests/stop_lane.rs` (`*_launching_session_*`).
